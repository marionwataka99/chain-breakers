Every outbreak is a chain. Prevention breaks it.

Germs spread from person to person through a series of links. Break one link and the spread stops.

I care about public health because many illnesses can be prevented when people have the right information. I built Chain Breakers to make prevention easy to understand and share.

Chain broken

One handwash, one vaccine, one bed net: each can stop the chain.

The six links of the chain of infection

Health workers use this model to decide where to act. Each prevention measure targets one link.

  1. A virus, bacterium, parasite or fungus that can cause disease.

    Break it with: treatment, disinfecting surfaces, boiling or treating water.

    Did you know? Most germs are too small to see without a microscope, so hands can look clean and still carry them.

  2. People, animals, dirty water, soil or food where the germ survives and multiplies.

    Break it with: clean water, safe sanitation, treating people who are sick.

    Did you know? Cholera bacteria spread through water contaminated with stool, which is why treating drinking water prevents outbreaks.

  3. Coughs, sneezes, stool, blood or other body fluids.

    Break it with: covering coughs, using toilets or latrines, safe disposal of waste.

    Did you know? Germs can survive on surfaces for hours or days, which is why cleaning and disinfecting are important.

  4. Hands, droplets in the air, contaminated food or water, or insects such as mosquitoes.

    Break it with: handwashing with soap, sleeping under treated bed nets, safe food handling.

    Did you know? Mosquitoes can transmit diseases like malaria and dengue, which is why using insecticide-treated bed nets is important.

  5. The mouth, nose, eyes or broken skin.

    Break it with: masks in crowded places, covering wounds, not touching your face with unwashed hands.

    Did you know? The flu virus can enter the body through the mouth, nose, or eyes, which is why washing hands and avoiding touching your face are important.

  6. A person without protection, especially babies, older people and those with weak immunity.

    Break it with: vaccination, good nutrition, regular health check-ups.

    Did you know? People with weakened immune systems, such as those with HIV/AIDS or cancer, are more vulnerable to infections.
